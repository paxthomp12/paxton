2025: Lost Myself

Following the 2024 season, I had an end-of-year meeting where the message was clear: we need you to throw harder. The target was 93+ mph. I took that feedback seriously and went to work with the intention of meeting their expectations.

In my attempt to add velocity, I made significant changes. I altered my mechanics, focused on adding weight to "get stronger," and centered everything around throwing harder. The results were the opposite of what we hoped for.

Metric 2024 2025 Change
Fastball Velocity 91.0 mph 89.9 mph -1.1 mph
Strikeout Rate 28% 18% -10%
Walk Rate 8% 9% +1%
Strike Rate 64% 62% -2%
CSW% 22% 16% -6%
Body Weight ~197 lbs 204 lbs +7 lbs

I wasn't the same pitcher. My mechanics felt different, my pitches didn't move the same way, and most importantly, I wasn't being myself on the mound. There were stretches where I competed well with what I had—moments where the competitor in me showed up—but I wasn't getting away with anything because my stuff had declined.

August 2025: The Breaking Point

In August, my body broke down. I ended the season on the Injured List. It was rock bottom. I had chased what others wanted, lost what made me effective, and paid the price physically.

The 2025 season taught me valuable lessons. I learned what it meant to lose yourself in pursuit of meeting external expectations. I learned that being a worse version of myself athletically—heavier, less explosive—made me worse at baseball. Most importantly, I learned that I needed to get back to being me.

Offseason 2025-2026: Becoming the Best Version

This offseason, I trained at Driveline Baseball with a fundamentally different approach. The goal wasn't to throw harder for the sake of throwing harder. The goal was to become a better athlete, rediscover my mechanical identity, and trust that if I did those things right, velocity would come naturally.

I started the offseason at 204 lbs—my heaviest playing weight from the 2025 season. Rather than trying to add more mass, I focused on becoming leaner and more explosive. DEXA scans tracked my progress and confirmed what I felt: I was becoming a better athlete.

My pitch shapes at Driveline mirror what worked in 2024—but now with 2-3 mph more velocity across the board. All four pitches (FF, CT, SW, CH) are moving the way they should because my mechanics are back to being mine, and I'm a more explosive athlete delivering them.

  • Sweeper: Sharp, late break away from righties—my highest stuff grade pitch

The Formula That Works

When you're yourself mechanically + you're a better athlete = everything clicks.

This isn't theory. The data proves it. My velocity is up, my strength metrics are up, my body composition is improved, and my pitch shapes are back to what made me effective in 2024. I'm not forcing anything. I'm just being the best version of myself.

What I Learned

You can't become someone you're not just because that's what others want. When I tried to add velocity by forcing it—changing my mechanics, adding weight, chasing a number—I lost everything that made me effective.

When I focused on being the best version of myself, the velocity came naturally. The lesson is clear: trust your identity, become a better athlete, and let your performance speak for itself.
